Before the Bang

What happened before the Big Bang, and what set it off? Nobody knows. But physicists have a handful of serious stories, and the next ten years of measurements will start to choose between them.

1 · What we actually know

The universe has a history we can read, back to a fraction of a second

13.8 billion years ago the universe was hot, dense and expanding. That much is not in doubt. We still see the afterglow (the cosmic microwave background, or CMB), we measure how much hydrogen and helium were cooked in the first minutes, and we watch galaxies fly apart.

What is not an observation is the famous "moment zero". That comes from running Einstein's equations backwards past the point where they stop working. A theory that predicts infinite density is telling you it has broken down, not that time began there.

2 · The first step back well supported

The "bang" was probably the end of a gigantic stretch

The leading idea is inflation. For a tiny fraction of a second, space expanded faster and faster, growing by a factor of about 10²⁶. When the field driving it decayed, its energy turned into the hot soup we call the Big Bang.

So inflation passed a real test. It just moves the question back: what came before inflation, and what started it?

3 · Seven stories all speculation

What came before? Seven serious answers

Each sketch is a picture of space and time together. Time runs upward, and the width is the size of the universe at that moment. The stories fall into three families.

There was no "before"

Time itself has a first edge, and asking what came earlier is like asking what is south of the South Pole.

No-boundary

Hartle & Hawking, 1983

Run time backwards and, near the beginning, it gradually turns into a fourth direction of space. The universe has a smooth rounded bottom, like the bottom of a bowl. There is no edge, and no moment of creation that needs a cause.

Test: it needs inflation to follow, so it rises or falls with inflation. Whether its maths is stable is still argued over.

Tunnelling from nothing

Vilenkin, 1982

Quantum particles can pass through walls they could never climb over; that is how some atomic nuclei decay. Here a whole tiny universe tunnels into existence from no space and no time, then inflates.

Catch: this "nothing" still comes with the laws of quantum physics. Test: like no-boundary, it stands or falls with inflation.

Something bigger and older

Our Big Bang is one local event inside a larger reality.

Eternal inflation

Vilenkin 1983; Linde 1986

Inflation never stops everywhere at once. It ends in patches, and each patch becomes a "bubble universe" with its own Big Bang. Ours is one of them, and new ones keep forming in the inflating sea around us.

Catch: Borde, Guth & Vilenkin (2003) showed that even this sea cannot be infinitely old. It pushes the beginning back without removing it.

Born inside a black hole

Smolin 1992; Popławski 2010

Matter falling into a black hole in a parent universe gets squeezed, rebounds, and opens a new expanding region on the other side. Our Big Bang would be the far side of someone else's black hole.

Test: Smolin's version predicts that our laws of physics are tuned to make lots of black holes, which astronomers can check.

An earlier universe

Our universe had a predecessor, and the Big Bang was a turning point rather than a start.

Big Bounce

Ekpyrotic: Khoury, Ovrut, Steinhardt & Turok 2001 · Loop quantum cosmology

An earlier universe was shrinking. At extreme density, quantum gravity makes space resist being crushed, like a ball that cannot be squeezed past a certain point. It rebounds into our expansion.

Test: the ekpyrotic version makes no primordial gravitational waves; detecting them would rule it out.

Conformal cyclic cosmology

Penrose, 2005 onward

Each universe ends cold, empty and infinitely big, with only light left. Light has no clock and no ruler, so "huge" and "tiny" stop meaning anything. Penrose argues that this dead end is mathematically the same as a new hot beginning.

Test: it predicts faint rings and spots in the CMB. Claims to have seen them are widely disputed.

CPT-mirror universe

Boyle, Finn & Turok, 2018

The Big Bang is a mirror. On the other side is an anti-universe, made of antimatter and running backwards in time. The two halves form one symmetric whole, so there was no event that needed a cause.

Test: it predicts no primordial gravitational waves and a massless lightest neutrino. Either measurement could rule it out.

4 · How we will find out

The deciding clue: ripples in space from the first instant

Inflation shakes space itself and should leave gravitational waves behind, imprinted as a faint swirl pattern in the polarisation of the CMB. Their strength is a number called r. Bounces and the mirror universe predict essentially none. So far nobody has seen them, which already rules out the simplest inflation models.

Two experiments will look much harder: the Simons Observatory in Chile, running now, and the LiteBIRD satellite, planned for the 2030s. Pick a possible result below and see which stories survive.

5 · The hardest part

How can there be no time?

We can't picture it directly, because picturing is itself something that happens in time. But nobody can picture curved four-dimensional space either, and analogies still get us surprisingly far.

Time is like temperature

Temperature is real, but it only exists for many particles together. Asking for the temperature of a single atom makes no sense. Time may be the same kind of thing: a collective property that works almost everywhere and fades near the beginning.

The film reel

A film shows motion, but the reel on the shelf has every frame present at once. "Time" in the film is just the order of the frames. The universe may be more like the reel than the projection.

The photograph with a clock

Take one still photo of a whole universe that contains a clock and a cup of coffee. Nothing in it moves. Yet in the photo, "clock says 3" goes with "coffee hot" and "clock says 4" with "coffee cold". Someone inside experiences change; from outside it is one frozen picture. Physicists Page and Wootters proposed this in 1983, and a toy version was demonstrated with entangled photons in 2014.

And space?

Space doesn't have to disappear along with time. In several approaches to quantum gravity, space-like structure remains after time is gone. In others, both grow out of something more basic, such as how strongly pieces of the universe are quantum-entangled.

6 · A bet personal guess

Where I would put my money, for now

  • ~70%The hot Big Bang was the end of inflation. The data put mild pressure on some of the simplest versions, not on the idea.
  • 1stBefore inflation, time itself fades out. The basic equation for the whole universe (the Wheeler–DeWitt equation) contains no time at all; time appears only inside the universe, as clocks become correlated with everything else. "Before" and "cause" then stop working near the beginning, like "south" at the Pole.
  • 2ndA bounce. It is the conservative option: the universe keeps existing, and quantum gravity just prevents infinite density.
  • ∞Every story hits the same wall. Even "from nothing" assumes the laws of quantum physics already exist. Physics explains how things change under laws; it cannot yet say why there are laws at all.
